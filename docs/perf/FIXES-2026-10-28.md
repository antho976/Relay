# Fixes 2026-10-28: the audit's regressions (wave 3)

The 2026-10-28 audit named six performance regressions and one red check. Each was measured
before anything changed, on the same machine and build profile as the change after it. Five
moved; the start screen's moved only between pulses, and the cost that remains is named with
its options.

Machine: Ryzen 7 9800X3D (16 threads), rustc 1.99.1 (Arch), GTK 4.24.1, NVIDIA (Vulkan
renderer), **dev profile** (`opt-level` 1, dependencies at 3). `perf_event_paranoid` is 2:
`perf stat` counts user-space instructions, `perf record` is refused, so nothing here is
sampled. Other sessions were building throughout (load 4–18), so wall times are noisy and the
instruction counts are the steadier column. The engine rows are three runs of
`perf run … --counters`, median shown. The client ran inside a private `kwin_wayland --virtual`
(1440×900) on `scripts/perf/native-baseline.py`'s fixture. Raw runs:
`runs/2026-10-28-wave3/` (`before/`, `after-*/`, `redact-bench/`, `ratchet/`).

## The PTY's key scan: fixed

Since 8b05970 every byte a terminal prints passed through `Redactor::redact` one at a time, a
state machine looking for `sk-ant-`. Now a byte search (`memchr`) for `s` and a substring
search (`memchr::memmem`) for the prefix jump to where a key can start; the byte walk runs only
where a key is open, after a prefix, and over a read's last six bytes. A test checks the search
against the byte walk on every two-way split of six awkward streams (`sk-sk-ant-`,
`ssk-ant-`, a key at a read's edge, a prefix as the last bytes).

| `path.pty.burst_1mib` (10 000 lines of 100 bytes) | instructions |
|---|---:|
| before | 30.90 M |
| after | **17.98 M** (−42 %) |
| with no redaction at all (the floor, measured once with the call removed) | 16.35 M |

The fixture's lines are `x`s, so most reads have no `s` at all. On real text the scan alone,
counted with `perf stat` on a copy of both versions (`redact-bench/`, relay-core's own sources
cut into reads):

| per MiB | old | new |
|---|---:|---:|
| 4 KiB reads | 15.73 M | **1.09 M** (−93 %) |
| 100-byte reads | 15.85 M | 3.91 M |

## Park: fixed

`kill_all` slept 50 ms before its first look at the process groups it had sent SIGTERM, so a
park, a close and shutdown each waited at least 50 ms even for a shell gone in one. The looks
now back off from 1 ms, doubling to 50 ms, and never sleep past the deadline.

| `op.session.park` | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| wall, p50 | 50.21 ms | **1.25 ms** |
| instructions | 0.49 M | 0.48 M |

`op.session.close` carries the same wait. It read 133–203 ms in the audit's morning run and
71–87 ms now, but those runs were under a different load, so this is not a clean before and
after.

Not done: moving the kill onto `proc::terminate_group` from #168. That helper works on one group
and sleeps a fixed `POLL_MAX` between looks. `kill_all` signals every PTY at once against one
shared deadline (RA-311).

## `bus.*` under the store lock: fixed

`bus.ping`, `bus.schema`, `bus.ops` and `bus.whoami` were plain `register`. They ran inside a
transaction holding the store mutex, and the schema and the registry walks grow with the op
count. They are `register_unlocked` now. Their few reads (the session, its guardrail config and
plugins, the project's name) happen in one `ctx.read`; rendering and the registry walks run
outside the lock. `session.bootstrap` was already unlocked.

A new row guards it: `path.session_list.during_bus_schema` issues a `session.list` one
millisecond into a whole `bus.schema` on another thread.

| | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| `session.list` beside a whole `bus.schema`, wall p50 (three runs) | 10.15 / 7.31 / 5.06 ms | **0.13 / 0.11 / 0.12 ms** |
| `bus.schema` whole, instructions | 58.94 M | 58.93 M |
| `bus.ops`, instructions | 8.16 M | 8.15 M |
| `bus.ping`, instructions | 14.7 k | 7.8 k |

The work itself is unchanged. What changed is that nothing waits behind it.

## The sidebar on agent events: fixed

Since a6701d2 every active-project agent's name, state and intent was hashed into the sidebar's
signature, so each change cleared and rebuilt every workspace, project and agent row. The
signature now holds only what moves rows: the registry, folding, the filter, open counts,
projects with an agent waiting, lamps on folded groups, and the agents' names. Agent rows (lamp,
intent, tooltip) and project lamps are updated in place from widgets the last rebuild kept, and
a lamp's classes change only when they differ.

With the sidebar fixed, most of what remained was the terminal panes. `reconcile` rebuilt a
pane's action keys on every change of its signature, and intent is in that signature. Each
header update also removed and re-added the lamp classes on the pane's root, which restyles the
whole pane, terminal included. Actions are now rebuilt only when something other than the
intent changed, and the classes change only when they differ.

`native-baseline.py --burst-only` gained `burst-intent-200` (200 `session.intent`, 20 ms apart)
and `--burst-page`. GTK thread, user-space instructions per event over an idle window:

| row | page | before | sidebar in place | and panes |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `session.intent` | Agents | 40.56 M | 8.33 M | **2.93 M** (−93 %) |
| `session.intent` | Board | 39.53 M (audit) | 6.97 M | **1.89 M** |
| `session.update {allow_ui}` | Agents | 0.46 M | 0.51 M | 0.52 M |

Task-clock for the 200 intents on Agents: 668 ms → 101 ms. What remains over `allow_ui` is
relaying out the two labels whose text changed.

## The start screen: the tick stops between pulses, and the pulse stops when nobody is there

The stage's tick callback returned `Continue` on every frame for as long as the screen was
open. It now stops when nothing moves (the opening, a ripple, the pointer's light easing, a
pulse), and a timeout brings it back for the next pulse. Pointer motion and clicks bring it back
at once. The frame log confirms it: before, 40–60 frames every second; after, none between
pulses.

Steady window of 29 s (about six pulse periods), default renderer:

| start screen at rest | CPU | wakeups/s |
|---|---:|---:|
| before | 30.2 % | 326 |
| after | 28.7 % | 367 |
| pulses switched off, old tick | 0.31 % | 302 |
| pulses switched off, new tick | **0.07 %** | **241** |
| Agents page (the renderer's floor) | 0–1 % | 240–242 |

So the tick change takes the screen to the renderer's floor between pulses (−61 wakeups/s), but
that was never the 16–30 %. **The pulse is the cost.** Every 4.8 s a wave crosses for 2.6 s, and
each of its frames redraws the whole 1440×900 field in Cairo on the CPU: a dot grid with a
`hypot`/`exp` per dot per wave, pushed to a group and masked with a gradient. The two 8 s runs
each side read 21 % and 27 % before, 28 % and 16 % after: an 8 s window lands on one or two
pulses, so its CPU is the phase.

### The pulse runs only while it can be seen and someone is there

The owner's call: keep the pulse, but run it only while the window is focused and on screen and
someone has used it in the last 10 s. Pointer motion, keys, clicks and the window regaining
focus count as input. The pulse runs on a clock of its own that stands still while paused
(`PulseClock`, with a test), so a resumed pulse continues from the phase it stopped at. A wave
caught mid-crossing stays drawn where it stopped. Drawing the field on the GPU stays open as
PLAYBOOK lead 22.

`native-baseline.py --pages start --seconds 29` in a private `kwin_wayland --virtual`, the
window on screen, before (the pulse always on) and after (`runs/2026-10-28-wave3/pause-*`):

| start screen, 29 s steady window | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| idle: focused, no input | 20.0 % CPU, 331 wakeups/s | **0.07 %, 242** |
| someone there: the pointer moves every 2 s | 29.6 %, 328 | **11.9 % / 15.0 %** (two runs), 287 / 300 |
| minimized | 0.03 %, 242 | 0.07 %, 242 |

With someone there the pulse still runs, and it costs less than before. The mark (the logo the
waves leave from) was redrawn on every frame of a pulse, though it changes only while its beat
swells, the first 420 ms of each. It is now redrawn only then. Its look depends on nothing else,
so the frames it skipped drew the same image.

Minimized was already free (GTK sends a suspended window no frames). The change there is that a
restore resumes the paused phase instead of jumping. 242 wakeups/s is the Vulkan renderer's
floor on every page.

**Unfocused was not measured.** In the virtual compositor the window keeps focus. A second
window opened for the purpose (a full-output `zenity`) is put behind it by KWin's
focus-stealing prevention, and fake-input clicks still land on Relay, so `is_active()` never
went false (a temporary log of it read `true` on every frame). The unfocused path is the same
pause as the idle and minimized ones, gated on `Window::is_active()` and resumed by its
`notify::is-active`, and is untested on a real desktop.

## The design ratchet: reset

Its ceilings were taken with rustc 1.99.0. On 1.99.1, 24 of 45 rows were over them by
1.55–4.88% (all 45 rows: +0.16 to +4.88%). The audit had already shown that the commit the
ceilings were set at reads 1–4.5% over on 1.99.1. Every ceiling is reset in a commit of its
own, and `ratchet.json` now records the compiler it was taken with. A check on another compiler
prints that first, so the next toolchain change reads as one.

## Also

- `engine.rs` takes its maps back after a panic poisoned them
  (`unwrap_or_else(PoisonError::into_inner)`). Before, every later keystroke panicked on
  `pty_ids`. There's a test.
- `test-exe-size` is fixed in #164 (Aide grew `all` from 246 MB to 252 MB).
- The redactor gained a test on a 4 KiB read with keys past offset 64, high bytes and invalid
  UTF-8 around them, so memchr's and memmem's vector paths are exercised. quick-narwhal's
  differential fuzz (200 000 buffers in random chunks against main's byte walk) found no
  difference.

## The terminal panes look the same

Terminal panes are not to be restyled. The `terminal.rs` and `app.rs` hunks change when a pane
restyles, not how it looks: the lamp classes end in the same set as before, and the actions are
rebuilt for every change except one of intent alone. The Agents page after the same burst
(`runs/2026-10-28-wave3/pane-look/`, before and after builds, virtual KWin, 1440×900) matches
pixel for pixel outside the header text rows. The only differences are the session names, which
the fixture picks at random, and the role label after each name, which moves with the name's
width. Lamps, the LIVE word, the keys, the terminal bodies and the frames are identical.
