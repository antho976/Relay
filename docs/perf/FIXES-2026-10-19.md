# Fixes 2026-10-19: a reopened thread read its on-screen pictures again

Journey: open a Threads conversation that holds pictures, leave it for another thread, come back.
Measured from `threads::open` to the moment every message picture on screen has its thumbnail,
with the conversation landed on its newest message. Budget: 100 ms (PLAYBOOK, "any page open").

Machine: Ryzen 7 9800X3D (16 threads), 30.5 GiB RAM, rust 1.99, dev profile (`opt-level` 1,
dependencies at 3), relay-native in a private `kwin_wayland --virtual` at 1440x900; load average
6–8 from peer sessions. Scenario: `scripts/perf/thumbs-reopen.py`, a disposable engine holding 30
messages of four 2000x1500 JPEGs (120 pictures; the thumbnail cache keeps 96), five runs per side,
not interleaved. Raw output in `runs/2026-10-19-thumbs/`.

## Why

The thumbnail cache (`threads_pictures.rs`) kept 96 entries and let go of the one *inserted*
longest ago, and a hit did not count as use. A thread drawn whole asks for its pictures oldest
first; the reads run newest first, since those are on screen. So the newest went into the cache
first and were the first to go: after the first opening it held pictures 3–98, and every reopening
read the 22 newest again, all eight on screen among them, two at a time. The first two reads of
any opening also went to the two oldest pictures, because reading started at the first ask.

## Change

- The cache lets go of the picture **asked for** longest ago, and an ask refreshes a kept one
  (`Kept`). A thread drawn whole keeps its newest pictures whatever order their reads finish in.
- Reading starts on the next idle, once the drawing has asked for every picture it shows, so the
  first reads are the newest too.

## Before and after

| Opening | Before (5 runs) | After (5 runs) |
|---|---|---|
| reopen: pictures on screen read again | 8 of 8 | **0 of 8** |
| reopen: ms until every on-screen picture shows | 138.9–151.7, median 148.6 | **18.0–25.3, median 18.6** |
| reopen: thumbnails read | 24 | 24 |
| cold: ms until every on-screen picture shows | 185.1–196.4, median 193.0 | 166.0–189.2, median 186.2 |
| cold: thumbnails read | 120 | 120 |

A reopening now shows its pictures from memory, at about 19 ms (the thread read and its layout),
inside the budget; before, it waited for its eight on-screen pictures to be read again, two at
a time, behind the two oldest. The cold opening's change is
inside the spread of five unpaired runs, so it is not claimed.

## Guardrail

`a_reopened_thread_keeps_the_pictures_on_screen` (`threads_pictures.rs`) replays the 120-picture
thread three times against the cache: each reopening reads exactly `120 - KEPT` pictures, all
from the top of the thread. Against the old cache it fails, naming pictures 96–119.

## Not done

A reopening still reads the 24 pictures past the cache's size at the top of the thread, off
screen, in the background. Reading thumbnails only as they scroll into view would end that; not
measured whether it is worth the change. Memory is unchanged: at most 96 thumbnails of 480 px.

# A pasted picture is made fit from its pixels

Journey: paste (or drag in) a picture into a Threads message box, until its chip is ready to go
up. Measured as the worker's time to make it fit (`prepare_pasted`), and the bytes that go up.

## Why

A pasted picture arrives decoded, as a texture. It was written out as a PNG at full size
(`save_to_png_bytes`), read back, and when it was past 2000 px or 4 MiB, written as PNG again by
gdk-pixbuf at the default compression, then as JPEG if that PNG was too heavy. A large photo paid
for two full-size PNG passes; a smaller one went up as a PNG of several MB. gdk-pixbuf's PNG writer
was also the slow part of every redrawn file: 261 ms for a 1600x1200 photo and 389 ms at
2000x1500, against 68 and 105 ms for GDK's own writer, for files about 1.5 % larger.

## Change

- A pasted picture's pixels are downloaded from the texture (RGB unless some pixel is not
  opaque), scaled to 2000 px, and written once: PNG when it packs under a byte a pixel (a
  screenshot, line art) or has transparency, else JPEG.
- Every PNG is written by GDK's writer (`png`), files' included.

## Before and after

`cargo test -p relay-native paste_cost -- --ignored --nocapture`, dev profile, the old path kept
beside the new for this run only and the two alternated, 5 runs each, median [min–max]. The three
wallpapers are the CachyOS ones under `/usr/share/wallpapers/cachyos-wallpapers/`
(`RELAY_PASTE_FILES`); raw output in `runs/2026-10-19-thumbs/paste-ab-dev.txt`.

| Pasted | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| photo `cachysurf3.jpg` 5120x2160 | 1167 ms [1151–1181], 2237 KB PNG | **200 ms** [199–208], **343 KB JPEG** |
| render `cachygalaxy99.jpg` 3840x2160 | 694 ms [691–703], 532 KB JPEG | **182 ms** [181–184], 532 KB JPEG |
| line art `Cachy_Topography.jpg` 3440x1440 | 451 ms [450–452], 842 KB PNG | **111 ms** [111–114], 867 KB PNG |
| synthetic photo 4032x3024 | 1004 ms [999–1026], 3018 KB PNG | **305 ms** [303–307], 2810 KB PNG |
| synthetic photo 1600x1200 | 89 ms [89–90], 2328 KB PNG | 91 ms [90–92], **596 KB JPEG** |
| synthetic screenshot 2560x1440 | 119 ms [118–121], 246 KB PNG | **81 ms** [80–82], 252 KB PNG |

The release profile reads the same (within 6 ms): the time is in libpng, libjpeg and gdk-pixbuf.
The synthetic 4032x3024 "photo" packs to 0.94 bytes a pixel once scaled down, under the PNG
threshold, so it stays PNG; the real photo does not.
`a_pasted_photo_goes_as_jpeg_and_a_screenshot_as_png` holds the choice of format.
